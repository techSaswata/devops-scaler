--- ROOT CAUSE ---

$ kubectl get deploy broken-image -n clinicflow-broken -o jsonpath='image: {.spec.template.spec.containers[0].image}{"\n"}'

  Tag v9.9.9-does-not-exist was never built. Note what the registry says:
  'may require authorization', NOT 'no such tag'. A registry will not
  confirm the absence of a possibly-private repository, so a typo and a
  permissions problem are indistinguishable from outside.

=== FAULT 2 of 4 — Pending, never scheduled ===

--- IDENTIFY ---
deployment.apps/broken-resources created

$ kubectl get pods -n clinicflow-broken -l fault=resources
NAME                                READY   STATUS    RESTARTS   AGE
broken-resources-7f75cf8dd4-pr6z2   0/1     Pending   0          21s
>> Pending. Not running-and-broken: never placed on a node at all.

--- INVESTIGATE ---

$ kubectl describe pod -n clinicflow-broken -l fault=resources | sed -n '/^Events:/,$p'
Events:
  Type     Reason            Age   From               Message
  ----     ------            ----  ----               -------
  Warning  FailedScheduling  21s   default-scheduler  0/2 nodes are available: 2 Insufficient cpu. preemption: 0/2 nodes are available: 2 No preemption victims found for incoming pod.

$ kubectl get nodes -o custom-columns='NODE:.metadata.name,ALLOCATABLE_CPU:.status.allocatable.cpu' --no-headers
ip-10-40-0-8.ap-south-1.compute.internal      1930m
ip-10-40-18-226.ap-south-1.compute.internal   1930m

--- ROOT CAUSE ---

$ kubectl get deploy broken-resources -n clinicflow-broken -o jsonpath='requests: {.spec.template.spec.containers[0].resources.requests}{"\n"}'
requests: {"cpu":"16","memory":"128Mi"}
  16 CPUs requested; a t3.medium has 2 allocatable. A request is a
  SCHEDULING CONTRACT, not a cap -- the scheduler will not overcommit it,
  so the pod waits indefinitely rather than starting and being throttled.

--- FIX ---

$ kubectl patch deployment broken-resources -n clinicflow-broken --type=json -p='[{"op":"replace","path":"/spec/template/spec/containers/0/resources/requests/cpu","value":"100m"},{"op":"replace","path":"/spec/template/spec/containers/0/resources/limits/cpu","value":"500m"}]'
deployment.apps/broken-resources patched

--- VERIFY ---

$ kubectl get pods -n clinicflow-broken -l fault=resources
NAME                                READY   STATUS    RESTARTS   AGE
broken-resources-76d8785c64-zhh5s   1/1     Running   0          31s

=== FAULT 3 of 4 — Running, but never Ready ===

--- IDENTIFY ---
deployment.apps/broken-probe created

$ kubectl get pods -n clinicflow-broken -l fault=probe
NAME                            READY   STATUS    RESTARTS   AGE
broken-probe-7ff6fd987d-6vnqz   0/1     Running   0          41s
>> STATUS Running with READY 0/1. The process is up; Kubernetes is
>> refusing to call it serviceable, so it joins no Service.

--- INVESTIGATE ---

$ kubectl describe pod -n clinicflow-broken -l fault=probe | grep -i 'readiness probe' | head -3
  Warning  Unhealthy  40s               kubelet            Readiness probe failed: Get "http://10.40.29.127:8000/healthz": dial tcp 10.40.29.127:8000: connect: connection refused
  Warning  Unhealthy  1s (x9 over 39s)  kubelet            Readiness probe failed: HTTP probe failed with statuscode: 404

--- ROOT CAUSE ---

$ kubectl get deploy broken-probe -n clinicflow-broken -o jsonpath='readiness path: {.spec.template.spec.containers[0].readinessProbe.httpGet.path}{"\n"}'
readiness path: /healthz
  /healthz is Go-ecosystem convention; this app serves /health and /ready.
  The probe did exactly what it was told. Proof the app is fine:
$ GET /health from inside the pod
  {"status":"healthy"}
$ GET /healthz from inside the pod
  HTTP 404 Not Found
>> The probe was right to fail. The PATH was wrong, not the application.

--- FIX ---

$ kubectl patch deployment broken-probe -n clinicflow-broken --type=json -p='[{"op":"replace","path":"/spec/template/spec/containers/0/readinessProbe/httpGet/path","value":"/health"}]'
deployment.apps/broken-probe patched
  waiting for the pod to become Ready . Ready after ~10s

--- VERIFY ---

$ kubectl get pods -n clinicflow-broken -l fault=probe
NAME                            READY   STATUS    RESTARTS   AGE
broken-probe-595d7bb4cd-rgkvs   1/1     Running   0          7s

=== FAULT 4 of 4 — a healthy pod that nothing can reach ===

--- IDENTIFY ---

$ kubectl get svc broken-service -n clinicflow-broken
NAME             TYPE        CLUSTER-IP       EXTERNAL-IP   PORT(S)    AGE
broken-service   ClusterIP   172.20.158.231   <none>        8000/TCP   17s

$ kubectl get endpoints broken-service -n clinicflow-broken
NAME             ENDPOINTS   AGE
broken-service   <none>      18s
>> ENDPOINTS <none>, while a healthy pod sits right there. No event, no
>> warning: the Service is reported as perfectly fine.

--- INVESTIGATE ---

$ kubectl get svc broken-service -n clinicflow-broken -o jsonpath='service selector: {.spec.selector}{"\n"}'
service selector: {"app":"brokenprobe"}

$ kubectl get pods -n clinicflow-broken -l app=broken-probe -o jsonpath='pod labels:       {.items[0].metadata.labels}{"\n"}'
pod labels:       {"app":"broken-probe","fault":"probe","pod-template-hash":"595d7bb4cd"}

--- ROOT CAUSE ---
  selector app=brokenprobe, label app=broken-probe. One
  hyphen. A Service builds its endpoint list purely by label match, and
  nothing warns about a selector that matches nothing. This is why 'the
  pod is Running' is never sufficient evidence that a service works.

--- FIX ---

$ kubectl patch svc broken-service -n clinicflow-broken --type=json -p='[{"op":"replace","path":"/spec/selector/app","value":"broken-probe"}]'
service/broken-service patched

--- VERIFY ---
  waiting for the endpoint to appear  populated after ~5s

$ kubectl get endpoints broken-service -n clinicflow-broken
NAME             ENDPOINTS           AGE
broken-service   10.40.30.215:8000   29s
$ GET / through the Service DNS name
{"status":"healthy"}
